   *Death and transformation* appears in **22 traditions (1,513 occurrences)**; *initiation* in **22 (770)**; *ascent* in **20 (484)**; *axis mundi* in **19 (544)**. At the level of broad motif families, cultures repeatedly organize meaning around passage through rupture, ordeal, altered status, and contact between planes. Jung would recognize a recurrent psychic morphology: not proof of inherited images in any strict biological sense, but evidence that human imagination returns to threshold experiences. Campbell would see the bones of initiation everywhere—while his better critics would insist that shared bones do not make every tradition a version of one story.

2. **“Sacred knowledge” is the Atlas’s great hub.**  
   It occurs **4,382 times across 21 traditions**, far beyond the next high-frequency families: *divine judgment* (**2,630**) and *hero journey* (**1,807**). More interestingly, it sits in the most recurrent sequence relations: *sacred exchange → sacred knowledge* occurs in **37 texts across 17 traditions**; the reverse, *sacred knowledge → sacred exchange*, in **35 texts across 16**; and *sacred knowledge → hero journey* in **35 texts across 16**. There is a real emerging picture here: knowing is not merely private illumination. It is received, traded, guarded, tested, enacted, and often turned into a journey. Corbin might have called this the difference between information and *gnosis*: knowledge that changes the knower.

3. **The strongest sequence signal may be a circuit, not an arrow.**  
   Because knowledge and exchange recur in both directions—**37 texts / 17 traditions** one way, **35 / 16** the other—the finding is not yet “exchange reliably produces revelation,” nor the reverse. It is a coupled pair: gift, vow, sacrifice, hospitality, teaching, theft, and revelation appear to form a narrative-economic loop. This is potentially more interesting than a clean Campbellian departure–initiation–return line. Myth may often move as reciprocity: what is received must be given, and what is given opens further knowing.

## What I distrust

1. **The sheer dominance of “sacred knowledge.”**  
   **4,382 occurrences** may disclose a profound cross-cultural concern—or it may be a capacious extractor bucket swallowing teaching, law, prophecy, counsel, cosmology, secret lore, and ordinary moral instruction. Test it by hand-auditing a stratified sample of its passages; compare its precision against narrower families; and split it into subtypes before treating it as one phenomenon.

2. **The sequence counts as evidence of narrative direction.**  
   The bidirectionality of exchange and knowledge is already a warning. With **5,763 extraction records** but only **54 texts with chains**, frequent motifs and long texts can generate many adjacent pairs without a meaningful grammar. Test against null models that preserve each text’s motif frequencies and chain lengths, then report effect sizes—not only recurrence counts. Count each text once, use mixed-effects models for tradition and source, and ask whether A→B is truly more likely than B→A.

3. **Independence at the level of traditions and texts.**  
   The corpus includes English translations, retellings, Project Gutenberg editions, comparative works, and multiple texts from related literary worlds. These are not independent witnesses. Also, tonight’s “maximally isolated” lineages cannot yet bear the evidential weight suggested here: their fresh raw labels and null-model results are not included in the displayed state. Test all broad claims with source-family and translator-aware resampling, then separately evaluate primary-language or community-curated materials. Isolation is not a magic seal; mediation matters.
