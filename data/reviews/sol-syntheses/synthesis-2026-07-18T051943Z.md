## 1. What is rising

### 1. A shared architecture of transformation is genuinely visible
**Death and transformation** and **initiation** occur in all **22 traditions** represented, with **1,513** and **770 occurrences** respectively. This breadth matters more than raw frequency: it suggests that traditions repeatedly organize experience around the passage from one condition of being into another.

Jung would recognize a candidate archetypal pattern—but would warn that an archetype is not proved by recurring labels. What may be universal is the psychic problem of irreversible change; its images remain culturally specific. Campbell would hear the deep rhythm of departure, ordeal, and renewal, while his critics would rightly ask whether “initiation” has become broad enough to absorb unlike rites and stories.

### 2. Wisdom appears not as possession, but as circulation
The strongest recurring relation is **sacred exchange → sacred knowledge**, found in **37 texts across 17 traditions**. Yet the reverse, **sacred knowledge → sacred exchange**, appears in **35 texts across 16 traditions**. This reciprocity is more interesting than a one-way sequence: gifts, sacrifices, hospitality, teaching, vows, and revelations seem to form an exchange circuit.

Jung might call this a pattern of psychic reciprocity: transformation requires commerce between ego and otherness. Campbell might see the boon given and returned. But the data resist a rigid monomyth: wisdom does not simply arrive at one prescribed stage—it moves back and forth.

### 3. The “journey” may be a knowledge-transforming ecology, not a universal plot
**Sacred knowledge → hero journey** occurs in **35 texts across 16 traditions**, while **hero journey → sacred knowledge** occurs in **35 texts across 15 traditions**. Knowledge also precedes **initiation** in **32 texts across 16 traditions**.

Together, these suggest a recurrent constellation: knowledge provokes passage; passage revises knowledge; initiation seals—or destabilizes—the result. Campbell would recognize the adventure’s call and boon, but the symmetry challenges any claim to a single universal order. The stronger finding may be a relational field rather than a canonical sequence.

### 4. The machinery is finding signal, but not yet consensus
Across readers, canonical Jaccard agreement ranges from **0.5653 to 0.6540**, while exact canonical matches reach only **35.45%–41.07%**. Canonicalization consistently improves agreement over raw labels, though only modestly. Tradition-level variation is substantial: Confucian material reaches **0.7562–0.8439**, while Celtic-Welsh falls as low as **0.4583**.

This is an important empirical result: the Atlas is neither hallucinating chaos nor measuring settled facts. It is measuring interpretable ambiguity.

## 2. What I distrust

### 1. The gravitational pull of “sacred knowledge”
It has **4,382 occurrences**, far above transformation (**1,513**) or initiation (**770**). A broad, frequent category will mechanically dominate both co-occurrence and sequence tables.

**Test:** rerun all relations with prevalence-controlled permutation nulls, inverse-frequency weighting, and narrower subfamilies of knowledge. Report lift or odds ratios, not counts alone.

### 2. Bidirectional “sequences” may be segmentation artifacts
Exchange/knowledge and journey/knowledge occur strongly in both directions. Long composite works—*Mahabharata*, *Arabian Nights*, *Metamorphoses*, Sufi anthologies—can generate both orders simply because motifs recur throughout them.

**Test:** vary passage windows, restrict to locally adjacent narrative episodes, normalize by text length, and hold out anthologies. A true grammar should survive those perturbations.

### 3. “Maximally isolated” does not yet mean evidentially independent
The visible Australian and Ainu sources are colonial-era English collections; the promised Inuit, San, Siberian, and Guiana raw labels are not present in this snapshot. Borrowing from Eurasian texts may be unlikely, but collector framing, translation vocabulary, deep diffusion, and extractor-imposed taxonomy remain live alternatives.

**Test:** conduct a preregistered holdout using indigenous-language or community-vetted editions, blind the extractor to tradition, and compare against culture- and genre-conditioned null models.
