# NDE Element Categories — Van Lommel's Prospective Study

The Lancet study of Pim van Lommel and colleagues prospectively followed survivors of cardiac arrest in Dutch hospitals and recorded which of ten distinct phenomenological elements the patients reported when an NDE occurred. The elements are described below as recurring features of the cohort's reports. Frequencies given for each element are those reported by the study among NDE-positive cases.

## Awareness of Being Dead

A clear, often startled awareness that one has died. The recognition is immediate and direct rather than inferred. The person knows the body is dead and that they are not the body. Roughly half of NDE-positive patients reported this element.

## Positive Emotions

A pervasive sense of well-being, peace, or joy. The emotional tone is overwhelmingly positive — anxieties, pain, and the urgency that surrounded the cardiac event are absent. For many patients this is the most striking feature of the experience: an emotional state qualitatively unlike any felt during ordinary life. Reported by a majority of NDE-positive cases.

## Out-of-Body Experience

The patient perceives themselves as separated from the physical body, often observing the resuscitation from a vantage point above the bed. Vision and awareness during this state are reported as continuous and clear. Some patients later describe specific actions of the resuscitation team or details of the room that were verifiable on follow-up.

## Moving Through a Tunnel

A sensation of moving through an enclosed dark space, usually with a light visible ahead or at the end. The motion is reported as smooth and irresistible, neither walking nor flying — a transit. Some accounts describe a tunnel, others a passage, a corridor, or a region of darkness traversed quickly.

## Communication with the Light

Encounter with a luminous presence experienced as conscious and personal. The communication is direct, often described as mind-to-mind, without spoken words. The light is felt to know the experiencer fully and to communicate something like complete acceptance, knowledge, or instruction.

## Observation of Colours

Perception of colors that are unlike any seen in ordinary waking experience — described as more vivid, saturated, or otherwise outside the normal visible spectrum. For some patients the colors are themselves a primary feature of the experience.

## Observation of a Celestial Landscape

Perception of an environment unlike any earthly place — described as a landscape, garden, meadow, or city of unearthly beauty. The environment is reported as both familiar and entirely new. There is often water, light, vegetation, or architecture, but the qualities are not those of ordinary places.

## Meeting with Deceased Persons

Encounter with people known to be dead — relatives, friends, acquaintances. They appear recognizable but in some cases younger or restored. Communication is direct. They are often felt as guides or greeters. In some cases the patient meets a deceased person whose death they did not yet know about, which is later confirmed.

## Life Review

A panoramic re-experiencing of the person's life. Events are seen all at once or in rapid sequence, sometimes from the perspective of others affected by them. The review is felt as understanding rather than judgment. A minority of patients in the prospective cohort reported this element.

## Presence of a Border

Awareness of a boundary or limit beyond which there is no return. The border may take many forms — a body of water, a fence, a line, a horizon. The experiencer recognizes that crossing it would be irrevocable. They are turned back, told to return, or know that they must.
