# Greyson NDE Scale — Sixteen Standardized Features

The Greyson NDE Scale is a sixteen-item instrument that classifies near-death reports into four phenomenological components: cognitive, affective, paranormal, and transcendental. The features below describe what each item is intended to capture, in the kind of phenomenological language the items were drawn from. The instrument is widely used to determine whether an experience meets the threshold to be considered a near-death experience and to characterize its profile.

## Cognitive Component

### Time Distortion

The experiencer reports that time passed differently — that subjective duration did not match clock time. Time may have seemed to stop, expand to a kind of eternal present, compress so that long sequences happened at once, or slow so that brief intervals contained vast amounts of awareness.

### Thought Acceleration

Thoughts move faster than is usual in waking life. Reasoning, recognition, and recall happen in compressed intervals. Many reports describe a vivid clarity and speed of mental operation that is felt as distinctly unlike ordinary cognition.

### Life Review (Panoramic Memory)

The whole or much of the life is seen at once or replayed in vivid sequence. Events are sometimes viewed from the perspective of those they affected. The review is comprehensive, accompanied by understanding rather than judgment.

### Sudden Understanding

A sudden grasp of large or fundamental matters — answers to questions one had not consciously held, or comprehension of how things fit together. The understanding is felt as direct rather than worked out, and as larger than any single insight.

## Affective Component

### Feeling of Peace

A pervasive sense of peace and well-being unlike any felt in ordinary life. Tension, fear, and pain dissolve. The state is felt as a baseline rather than a temporary mood.

### Feeling of Joy

A profound joy — sometimes described as bliss, ecstasy, or the most intense happiness ever experienced. Unlike ordinary joy it does not seem to depend on circumstances and is not followed by any sense of loss when remembered.

### Cosmic Unity

A felt sense of being one with the universe, with all life, or with everything that exists. Boundaries between self and other dissolve. The experiencer feels themselves continuous with the world rather than a separate observer of it.

### Encounter with Brilliant Light

Perception of a light that is exceedingly bright but not painful, often felt as conscious and personal. The light is described as warm, loving, and knowing. Communication with the light is direct.

## Paranormal Component

### Vivid Sensory Experiences

Sensory perceptions that are unusually clear, sharp, or saturated. Sights are more vivid than ordinary sight; colors are more intense; sounds are more distinct. The experience is felt as more real than ordinary reality, not less.

### Apparent Extrasensory Perception

The experiencer reports knowledge of events occurring at a distance, in adjacent rooms, or beyond visual range — knowledge that would not have been accessible to the body in its current state.

### Precognitive Vision

Awareness of future events — sometimes images of one's own future, sometimes glimpses of future events involving others. The visions are felt as direct knowledge rather than speculation.

### Out-of-Body Experience

The experiencer perceives themselves as outside the physical body, often viewing it from above. They see the resuscitation, the room, themselves on the bed. Vision is reported as panoramic and clear.

## Transcendental Component

### Otherworldly Environment

Perception of an environment unlike any earthly place — a landscape, region, or realm with its own character. The place is felt as real, distinct, and somehow more substantial than ordinary settings.

### Mystical Entity

Encounter with a being recognized as sacred or spiritual — described as a guide, a sacred presence, an angel, or a religious figure depending on the experiencer's background. The encounter has a numinous quality.

### Visible Spirits of the Dead

Encounter with deceased persons known to the experiencer — recognized as themselves, often present to greet, guide, or communicate something specific.

### Border or Point of No Return

Awareness of a limit beyond which return would not be possible. The boundary is recognized as decisive. The experiencer is turned back, told to return, or chooses to return.
