# IANDS Phenomenological Consensus Description

The International Association for Near-Death Studies has, across decades of accumulated research, converged on a description of recurring phenomenological features in near-death reports. The features below summarize that consensus. They are not stages — most experiences include only some — but the same features recur across cultures and across decades of independent reports.

## Sense of Being Dead

The experiencer is aware that they have died. The recognition is direct, not inferred. There is sometimes mild surprise or detached interest, rarely fear. The person knows themselves to be themselves but no longer the body that lies on the bed.

## Peace and Painlessness

Pain is absent. A profound peace pervades the experience. The peace is described as deeper than any felt in ordinary life — an absence of striving, anxiety, and the usual undertone of effort. Many report it as the defining tone of the experience.

## Out-of-Body Experience

The person perceives themselves apart from the physical body, often above it, watching the resuscitation, watching the room, watching themselves. Vision during this state is described as clear, panoramic, and unconfined by walls. The person feels themselves to have a body but it is light, weightless, and not subject to ordinary location.

## The Tunnel Experience

The experiencer moves through a dark enclosed space toward light. The space is variously described as a tunnel, a corridor, a valley, a cylinder, or an undifferentiated darkness through which the person travels. Movement is rapid, smooth, and felt as drawing rather than self-propulsion.

## People of Light

Encounter with luminous beings other than the central being of light. They are felt as personal, conscious, present. They greet, accompany, or guide the experiencer. Some are recognized as deceased loved ones; others are felt as spiritual beings whose specific identity is less important than their presence.

## The Being of Light

Encounter with a central luminous presence felt as personal, knowing, and overwhelmingly loving. The light is brilliant but does not hurt to look at. Communication with it is direct, without speech. The experiencer feels themselves fully known and fully accepted. The being is sometimes identified by religious frame (Christ, an angel, God) but the underlying phenomenology is the same: a presence of love and complete understanding.

## The Life Review

A comprehensive review of the person's life unfolds in the presence of the being of light. Events appear all at once or in rapid sequence. Significant moments stand out. The experiencer often sees themselves from the perspective of others affected by their actions. The review is accompanied by understanding rather than condemnation.

## Reluctance to Return

The experiencer is reluctant to return to the body. The state of peace, light, and union is felt as one's true home. Return feels like a heavy reabsorption into a smaller, denser, more constrained existence. Some are sent back with explicit instruction; others choose to return for the sake of children or unfinished tasks.

## Personality Transformation

After the experience, the person's life changes. Old anxieties recede. Materialism, status concerns, and competitive striving lose their hold. Relationships, learning, kindness, and contemplative practice take on central importance. The transformation is not a momentary inspiration but a lasting reorganization of values.

## New Views of Death

The fear of death is gone or sharply reduced. Death is felt as a transition rather than an end. This is not adopted as a doctrine but held as direct knowledge from what was experienced. Many experiencers become unusually able to comfort the dying and the grieving.
