# Mystical Experience Questionnaire — MEQ30 Dimensions

The MEQ30 is a thirty-item self-report instrument developed to measure the occurrence and intensity of mystical-type experiences, particularly under psychedelic conditions. Its items load onto four dimensions identified through factor analysis: Mystical (encompassing several historically distinct features), Positive Mood, Transcendence of Time and Space, and Ineffability. The dimensions below describe the kinds of experience each is intended to capture.

## Mystical Dimension — Internal Unity

A felt sense that everything within consciousness is one. Distinctions between thoughts, feelings, perceptions, and the self that is having them dissolve. There is no inner observer separate from what is observed; the entire interior of awareness is unified.

## Mystical Dimension — External Unity

A felt sense that everything external to ordinary self — the room, the world, all life, the cosmos — is one. The experiencer perceives themselves as continuous with what they had taken to be outside them. The sense of a separate self looking out at a separate world is gone; what remains is one undivided field.

## Mystical Dimension — Noetic Quality

The experience is felt as containing direct knowledge — a kind of knowing that does not depend on inference. What is perceived is felt to be the way things really are, more truly than ordinary perception conveys. This is not held as a guess or a belief but as immediate cognition of reality.

## Mystical Dimension — Sacredness

The state, the perceived reality, and what is encountered within the experience are felt as sacred. There is reverence, sometimes awe, sometimes gratitude. The sacredness is not connected to a particular religious framework but is recognized directly as a quality of what is being perceived.

## Mystical Dimension — Transcendence

The experiencer transcends the ordinary categories of self, time, and space. They feel themselves to encounter or to be united with something greater than the personal self. Religious traditions name this differently — Ground of Being, the Absolute, ultimate reality — but the experience itself does not depend on the name.

## Positive Mood — Joy and Bliss

A profound positive feeling pervades the experience — joy, bliss, ecstasy. The state is qualitatively unlike ordinary good moods; many experiencers describe it as the most intensely positive feeling of their lives.

## Positive Mood — Peace and Tranquility

A pervasive deep peace, beyond ordinary calm. Anxieties dissolve. The person rests in a quietness that does not depend on circumstances and is not disturbed by what arises in awareness.

## Positive Mood — Awe

A sense of awe in the presence of what is being perceived. The awe combines wonder, reverence, smallness, and an opening of perception to a magnitude that ordinary attention would not have made room for.

## Positive Mood — Tenderness and Love

Overwhelming feelings of love directed toward all beings, the world, and being itself. The love is not sentimental — it is unconditional and uniformly distributed, not contingent on the qualities of its objects. Tears of recognition or gratitude are common.

## Transcendence of Time

Ordinary time is suspended. The experience feels timeless, or eternal, or to encompass all time at once. There is no sense of duration. Past, present, and future may be perceived simultaneously, or the entire experience may take place in something other than sequential time.

## Transcendence of Space

Ordinary space is suspended. The experiencer feels themselves to be everywhere, nowhere, beyond location, or to occupy a space that has different rules. The boundaries that ordinarily contain experience within a particular place are gone.

## Ineffability — Inadequacy of Language

What was experienced cannot be put into words. Ordinary language refers to ordinary categories, and what was perceived was outside those categories. The experiencer is left feeling that their best descriptions fail to convey the actual content. The inadequacy is felt as a property of language itself, not as a failure of the speaker's vocabulary.

## Ineffability — Reality Beyond Concepts

The state contains a recognition that what was perceived stands behind or beyond the categories the mind ordinarily uses to organize experience. This is felt as a structural feature of what was encountered: it is not the kind of thing concepts can hold.
